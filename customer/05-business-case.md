## Problem and economic basis

Source: [discovery Q-01/Q-02](01-discovery-record.md). Eight fictional bearing-related stops, 80 hours/year at $2,500/hour = $200,000 annual downtime loss. An additional $48,000 repair/parts/overtime is stated as excluded from that downtime rate, giving $248,000 illustrative combined annual loss. These are invented scenario inputs, not measured financial evidence. Finance must verify allocation and overlapping costs before reliance.

The 30% reduction goal applies to bearing-related stops, not automatically to downtime hours, repair expenditure or the whole 60-motor fleet. Pulse initially covers 12 motors; their share of historical events, losses and production exposure is TBD. No savings projection can be attributed to Pulse from the fleet-wide loss figures alone.

ROI = (attributable benefits − lifecycle costs) / lifecycle costs over the same evaluation period and price basis. Payback is the first point cumulative net cash flow recovers investment; state if it never does. Both are **TBD**, pending supported costs and benefits.
