# Systems Engineering Standards Assurance

## Start from the existing engagement

Read the current customer discovery, scope, requirements, constraints, risk, decision-authority, and lifecycle records. Preserve IDs and accepted baselines. Reuse existing artifacts; do not restart discovery or duplicate records merely to invoke this skill.

Read [Standards assurance reference](references/standards-assurance-standard.md), sections 1–5, at the start of an applicability or SEMP review. Load the relevant later sections for each assessed domain. Its edition list is a supplied baseline; verify current publication metadata with official publishers when choosing or revisiting editions. Retain the governing contractual edition unless an authorized change adopts another.

Resolve related skills by their exact frontmatter names using the available skill catalog; use their actual returned package/path to read them. If absent from a cached catalog, search active personal skills by frontmatter name. Do not silently enable disabled skills or treat a project copy as an installed dependency.

- `customer-delivery-service-operations`: engagement scope, customer decisions, acceptance, service and exit.
- `build-production-ai-systems`: technical architecture, implementation and engineering evidence.
- `dme-diagram-standard`: every new or revised diagram.

If a dependency is unavailable, name it and record the affected handoff as unresolved; continue useful authorized work that does not depend on it. Do not claim all skills were applied.

## Execute the assurance workflow

1. Identify the system of interest, lifecycle scope, affected people, criticality, customer obligations, and technical authority.
2. Determine applicable system, software, architecture, requirements, V&V, information, quality and AI-risk guidance. Apply domain overlays only with an explicit basis.
3. Record edition, applicability, ownership, tailoring rationale, approver, source and review trigger. Separate project, customer, supplier and organization responsibilities.
4. Prepare or update lifecycle tailoring and the SEMP. Define reviews, decision criteria, configuration control, integration, V&V, transition, operation and retirement responsibilities.
5. Review requirements for necessity, atomicity, clarity, feasibility, traceability and verifiability. Preserve parents and derive children when needed.
6. Identify architecture stakeholders, concerns, viewpoints, model kinds, views and correspondence rules. Assess semantic consistency; do not require a fixed diagram count.
7. Match V&V methods and independence to risk. Assess applicable product-quality characteristics and AI risks, including abstention, false alerts, data validity and human authority.
8. Map obligations to actual controls, artifacts and evidence. Assign unresolved gaps an owner, affected gate, remediation and required closure evidence.
9. Return the assessment to the engagement and technical owners, with precise maturity and next action. Revisit at requirements/architecture reviews, reference/site validation, ORR, acceptance, changes and retirement.

## Assure lifecycle cost and affordability

Review the single governed project lifecycle cost model referenced by `customer-delivery-service-operations` and `build-production-ai-systems`; do not generate a competing estimate. Include cost-review responsibilities, cadence and decision criteria in lifecycle tailoring/SEMP. Assess coverage from discovery through retirement, evaluation period, currency/price basis, workload, cost ownership, sources/dates, formulas, exclusions, uncertainty, contingency and sensitivity. Check one-time/recurring separation, actuals versus forecasts, double counting, consistent architecture comparisons and separate benefit evidence. Check inflation/discount treatment where used; do not invent financial assumptions or customer approval.

Assess the cost baseline at discovery/business-case review where this skill is engaged, scope/hosting and architecture reviews, pre-build commitment, deployment/ORR, acceptance, agreed operational reviews, material changes, renewal/transfer and retirement. Record artifact/version, findings, affordability disposition, responsible owner, approval authority, unresolved conditions and required closure evidence. Require evidence appropriate to maturity: estimates may support planning, measured costs support actual-cost claims, and fictional approval establishes only a simulated disposition. Block only decisions dependent on material unresolved cost risks. Return findings through existing assurance and customer handoffs; do not claim standards conformity merely because a cost model exists.

## Evidence and completion rules

Distinguish proposed plans, mapped controls, implemented controls, tested evidence and formal assessment. A source URL or crosswalk row does not establish conformity. Cite only clauses actually inspected; describe public publisher summaries as summaries. Never invent independence, customer approval, certification, test results or a passed gate.

For fictional portfolio exercises, label scenario assumptions and simulated dispositions. They may support a planning baseline but cannot establish real site readiness or independent assessment. Keep incomplete evidence visible at the affected gate; continue other authorized work.

Respect the user's current scope and stop instructions. Reuse recorded authorization rather than repeatedly requesting it. Prepare a concrete review package before any approval that is genuinely required. Call assurance complete only for the stated lifecycle state and verified criteria, with gaps and limitations identified.
